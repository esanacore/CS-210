# SNHU Privacy And Portfolio Guide

This guide uses a conservative sharing policy for coursework archives. It is not legal advice; when in doubt, check current SNHU policy, course instructions, or an instructor/career advisor.

## Default Position

Preserve and showcase your own work, but do not assume school-provided material can be republished.

Treat these as restricted unless sharing is clearly allowed:

- full assignment prompts
- rubrics and grading guides
- instructor feedback and private comments
- solution keys or starter files not meant for redistribution
- LMS screenshots or course pages
- documents containing student IDs, private emails, or account identifiers
- other students' names, work, or comments

## Employer-Safe Options

Use one of these approaches when you want to show growth without overexposing course material:

- Public repo with sanitized README, your authored source, build/run instructions, reflection, and a short project summary.
- Private repo for complete artifacts, shared directly with a specific employer if appropriate.
- Redacted PDF or portfolio page that explains the scenario and your implementation without copying assignment text.
- Public “modernized” follow-up that reimplements the idea using your own requirements, separate from the original submission.

## Suggested Public Wording

```text
This repository preserves my own coursework implementation and later documentation polish.
Course-provided prompts, rubrics, templates, feedback, and other third-party materials are
not relicensed here and may be omitted, summarized, or kept private to respect school policy.
```

## Practical Checklist

Before making a coursework repo public:

- Remove student IDs and private identifiers.
- Replace copied assignment text with a short original summary.
- Summarize rubric/feedback instead of copying it verbatim.
- Keep grade reports, full feedback, and course documents private when policy is unclear.
- Make the README clear about what is original submission work versus later polish.
- Keep a private branch or private backup with full artifacts if you want to preserve them.

- [SNHU Student Handbook](https://pages.snhu.edu/students/handbooks/Student_Handbook.pdf)

The practical reading is: students may own the work they create, but that does not automatically grant permission to republish course-provided text, rubrics, third-party readings, LMS content, or private feedback. Use public summaries and private full archives when the boundary is unclear.
